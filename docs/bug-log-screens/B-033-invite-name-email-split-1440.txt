B-033: M1/A2, Invite people box, /org/members, admin Tester M One at "M1 Sweep Co 1791387231"
(a fresh organisation made for this sweep), browser, 1440 width.

The invite box's own placeholder reads:
  "Faith Kerubo, faith@yourcompany.com
   Joseph Kiprono, joseph@yourcompany.com"
i.e. it documents "Name, email" on one line as the way to invite someone with their name.

Typing exactly that format for one person:
  "M1 Member Test, m1member.1791387231@sweeptest.test"
produced a live preview reading:

  "1 person will be invited. 1 line is not an email address."
  m1 member test            Not an email address
  m1member.1791387231@sweeptest.test   Will be invited

So the single "Name, email" line was split on the comma into two separate invitee rows: the name
half ("m1 member test", already lower-cased) was rejected as "Not an email address" and silently
dropped, and only the email half was queued to be invited, as an invite with no name attached.

Pressing "Send 1 invitation" confirmed this: the person now appears in the People table as
  "M1member"   m1member.1791387231@sweeptest.test   Invited, not opened
with "M1member" (not "M1 Member Test") as their display name, clearly derived from the email's
local part rather than typed by anyone. The invitation email itself opens "Hi M1member," -
the garbled, nobody-typed name is what the invited person actually reads.

This directly touches M1's requirement that nothing forces confusion about who is being invited
or how: the one documented way to give an invited person's name does not work, and what reaches
them is an invented-looking name built from their email address, not the words the admin wrote
and not blank either (B-004 is a visually similar but different defect: there, email shows where
a name belongs in a confirmation list; here, the system fabricates a name from the email rather
than showing the raw email or the typed name).

Network: no 4xx/5xx on the invite submission itself (POST returned 200); the defect is in the
one-line parser, not a failed request.

Severity: Wrong information (the product writes a name nobody wrote, including in an email a
real person reads), bordering on Confusing (the documented input format silently fails).
