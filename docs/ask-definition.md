# What Ask is

Decided by Hafsah on 5 October 2026 (decision 100).

## Its name

Ask is named for the organisation: "Ask Coamana", "Ask NABG". The same name is used on every screen, on WhatsApp and in emails. It is called "Ask Groundwork" only where there is no organisation yet, for example before somebody has signed up.

Under the name, it says:

> Ask about the company, your work or your check-ins.
>
> You can also add context or documents to your check-ins here.

## A check-in and Ask are two different things

**A check-in** is the scheduled conversation about your work. What you say goes into the record and into the reports, and the people in that check-in can read it.

**Ask** is there any time. **It covers the whole organisation and your own work.** One check-in is a
focus you can pick when you want one; it is never what Ask is about (Hafsah, 5 October).

You can use it to:

- ask about the company, your work or your check-ins;
- add context or a document to one of your check-ins;
- ask for leave, or ask to spend money.

Asking with no check-in picked is the ordinary case, not a special one.

## What stays private

What you ask in Ask stays private. Nobody else reads your questions.

Anything you add to a check-in through Ask is different. Ask says it back to you, and you confirm it first. Once you confirm, it becomes part of that check-in, and the people in that check-in can read it, as with anything said in the check-in itself.

A request for leave or spending goes to the person who approves it, as it does today.

## The first time you open it

The first time somebody opens Ask, it tells them what it can do, in plain sentences:

> This is Ask Coamana. I answer about the whole organisation and about your own work, and you can point me at one of your check-ins whenever you want to. Ask me about the company, about your work, or about your check-ins. You can add context or a document to one of your check-ins here, and ask for leave or to spend money. What you ask me stays private. Anything you choose to add to a check-in works differently. I say it back to you first, it goes in only once you say yes, and from then on it is part of that check-in.

## The suggestions under it

Six, general and not one set per situation (decision 101, with the sixth added when Hafsah said the
suggestions must cover leave and spending too):

1. What did I say I would do?
2. What is my check-in for, and who reads it?
3. When is my next check-in?
4. What are the company's priorities right now?
5. Add a document or some context to my check-in.
6. Ask for leave, or to spend money.

Four of the six need a check-in to be about. Somebody with none is offered the two that do not.

## Where this is used

Every place that names or describes Ask follows this page:

- the prompt Ask runs on;
- its welcome and its empty states;
- its replies on WhatsApp;
- how a message is sent to the right part (a question, something added to a check-in, a request);
- emails that mention it.
