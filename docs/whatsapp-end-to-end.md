# WhatsApp end to end, for the first client (decision 81, item 1)

Read from the code on 2 October 2026, branch `wip/plain-reports`. Nothing was run against WhatsApp: no test talks to Meta, and nothing here was tried on a real number.

## What works today

- **Every kind of check-in the client needs works over WhatsApp:** a sales team's check-in, company planning (objectives), onboarding and probation, performance plans, recognition and renewals, and project check-ins. A person can open one, answer it, send a photo or a document, say "finish", and get the report back on their phone, in their own language.
- **Ask Groundwork works over WhatsApp, but only when the person has no check-in open.** It is the same Ask Groundwork as in the app: the same questions, memory, "what did I say", to-dos, deadlines, and leave, hours and spending requests said back and confirmed with a yes before anything is written.
- **Leave, hours and spending requests** go to the right approver: the line manager if they lead or are an admin, otherwise an admin; spending over the limit goes to the person the rule names.
- **Safety:** every incoming message's signature is checked; "stop" stops messages; the same message twice gets one answer.

## What is missing, or works only in the app

1. **Fixed (2 October, decision 82 item 1).** When a person confirms a leave, hours or spending request, the one who approves it is told on WhatsApp ("Hi Wairimu, Amina has asked for leave from Monday 5 October to Friday 9 October, and it is waiting for you. Reply here to approve or decline it, or open Groundwork to read it first."), and in the app on Today, as before. The approver can reply "approve" or "decline", also during one of their own check-ins; Groundwork says back what they are deciding ("You are approving Amina's leave from Monday 5 October to Friday 9 October. Is that right?") and decides only on a yes, through the boards' own code, which checks again that they are the one who approves. The person is then told ("Hi Amina, your request for leave from Monday 5 October to Friday 9 October was approved by Wairimu. Open Groundwork to read it."), whether it was decided in the app or by reply. The approver's message counts as Groundwork writing first; the person's does not, because it answers their own request. Hours can be approved but not declined, as on the board.
2. **Fixed (2 October, decision 82 item 2).** A person in a check-in can write "ask" (or "Ask Groundwork") and the messages after it go to Ask Groundwork, through its own entry; "back", or the check-in's name or number from the list, returns them. "switch" gives the list of open check-ins again. These words are said once, in one sentence, when a check-in starts on WhatsApp: "You can write "ask" at any time to ask Groundwork something, "back" to come back to your check-in, and "switch" to choose another check-in." A message during a check-in that is plainly a leave, hours or spending request ("I want leave on 20 October", "log 6 hours on Atlas", "I need to spend 12,000 on fuel") is asked about once, in Hafsah's words: "Shall I treat this as a leave request?" (or "as a spending request?", "as hours?"). Yes hands it to Ask Groundwork, which says it back and asks before anything is sent; no puts it in the check-in; anything else is asked again. "I spent 12,000 on fuel this week" or "I was on leave on Monday" stay in the check-in without a question. The waiting request is held for ten minutes in the running copy of the API only; see "What can only be tested once hosted".
3. **Fixed (2 October, decision 82 item 6).** A roster entry nobody linked, whose email is the address somebody signs in with, is that person for leave, hours and spending, and for who approves them. The addresses are compared trimmed and without regard to capitals, and only accounts in the same organisation count. An address on two unlinked entries, or a sign-in already linked to another entry, matches nobody. One place decides this (`suites/a-roster-entry-and-its-sign-in.ts`), and Ask Groundwork, the leave, hours and spending boards, and who approves all ask it.
4. **Performance management around the check-in is app-only:** the person's side, their appeal, the admin's decision on an appeal, and agreeing how a check-in ends.
5. **Fixed (2 October).** The "keep this check-in off the phone" setting is now kept everywhere: the session-ready message, the opening notice, the report sent to the phone when a session closes, and messages coming in. A message to a check-in kept in the app is not taken into it, and the person reads: "Your check-in "Performance plan for Amina" is kept in the Groundwork app, so I cannot take your answers to it here. Please open Groundwork and answer it there." Tested on a performance plan. **Ask Groundwork too (2 October, decision 82 item 7):** a question asked on WhatsApp never reads a check-in kept in the app. "What did Femi say", "what came of it", "think it through", "what did I commit to", "how am I doing", what is waiting, and the suggestions to talk about all leave such a check-in out in the read itself (the query never selects it, and its report is refused), so its words never reach the reply or the prompt the model is given. "What did I say" reads only what the person typed to Ask Groundwork, never a check-in. In the app, Ask Groundwork is unchanged. Tested by feeding a real kept-in-app check-in and asking each question both ways.
6. **Fixed (2 October).** One function tidies every number into the form WhatsApp sends, with a plus in front ("+2348012345678"), in Settings, in an admin's roster entry, and before every send. A local number starting with 0 takes the organisation's country, read from its own WhatsApp number or from the numbers already kept for its people when they all share one country code. With no country known it is refused, and the person reads: "Please write the whole number with the country code at the start, for example +234 801 234 5678." Every lookup matches a number already stored in either form, the whole number or the local one, and a local one only in its own country. The roster import (SCIM, and the people route through it) uses the same function too (decision 82, item 8), so an organisation's import is tidied, or refused with the same sentence, exactly like Settings.
7. **Fixed (2 October), with Hafsah's templates (decision 83).** Every session is announced on WhatsApp when it opens, the first one included, on Groundwork's number as on an organisation's own, with `checkin_open`: "Hi Miriam, your check-in for the sales team is open. It takes about five minutes, and you can answer right here. Reply START to begin." "Start" begins the check-in and "Not now" stops Groundwork writing first that day. Every morning, a person with a check-in not yet answered gets at most one of: `checkin_overdue` (once, only while it is still open), `checkin_due_tomorrow` (once), and on the lead's chase days, by the email reminder's own rule, `checkin_team_almost_in` (at least half in) or `checkin_your_view_matters` (fewer than half in, or a check-in about one person). `checkin_opens_soon` goes an hour before a session that opens at a set time. Groundwork writes first at most once a day, or twice on a day a check-in opens, checked in one place every one of these goes through. None goes to somebody on leave, after STOP or "Not now", or about a check-in kept in the app. Outside the 24 hours each goes as its template, and while the template is not approved it is recorded as not sent. `checkin_closes_soon` is not sent, because nothing closes at a set hour yet. `lead_daily_summary` is sent (2 October, decision 82 item 9): at seven each morning in Lagos, each lead with a check-in open gets one paragraph of who has checked in, who has not, and what waits for them to approve or decline, counting nobody not yet let in and saying nothing from a check-in kept in the app. Its template ends with a blank, which WhatsApp refuses, so a corrected version is proposed to Hafsah in `docs/whatsapp-templates.md`. The session-ready sweep no longer sends its own WhatsApp message, so nobody gets two for one session. The wording, with each template checked against WhatsApp's rules, is in `docs/whatsapp-templates.md`.
8. **Fixed (2 October).** A reply always goes from the number the person wrote to: a message to Groundwork's number is answered on Groundwork's number, even for an organisation with its own, and the report after a session closes goes on the number the person last wrote to. Every message that comes in is recorded with the number it came in on, and the 24-hour window is measured on the number a message goes from. An organisation with its own number gets its notices only from that number; Groundwork's number no longer sends it a second one.
9. **Fixed (2 October, decision 82 item 5).** The opening notice is now Hafsah's `checkin_open`, which never says "released with everybody else's", for any check-in. The three other places that said it (Ask Groundwork's start, Ask Groundwork's arrival message, and the embedded panel's due list) now say only "It takes about ten minutes." on a check-in about one person (2 October); team check-ins read as before.
10. **Suggested answers are dropped** on WhatsApp; boards and reports beyond the text summary say "There is more on this in Groundwork."
11. **Fixed (2 October, decision 82 item 4).** When WhatsApp is held by the paid add-on or the monthly limit, a person who wrote in the last 24 hours gets one free message, not counted towards the month, once in any 24 hours, for example: "Your organisation has not turned on WhatsApp, so this check-in stays in the app. You can carry on in the Groundwork app." Outside the 24 hours nothing can go at all; the send record keeps the same sentence for the app to show.
12. **Fixed (2 October).** On Groundwork's number, a number in two organisations can write "switch" to choose again; after choosing, the check-ins open in that organisation follow.

## What can only be tested once hosted

- The webhook handshake and the signature on the real body, for Groundwork's number and each organisation's own.
- Whether WhatsApp waits for a reply that takes several model calls.
- Hafsah's templates approved, in English, as Utility (see `docs/whatsapp-templates.md`, which says which may be refused and why). Line breaks are settled in the code (2 October): no blank ever holds a line break, a tab or a run of spaces, and every message fits WhatsApp's 1,024 characters. Outside the 24 hours the report goes as its name and one short line of where things stand, and the template itself says "Open Groundwork to read it."; inside the 24 hours it goes whole, or, where it would not fit one message, as that same short line. The session notice is `checkin_open`, which is one line.
- List taps, photo and document download with the organisation's token, and the relay through a customer's own system.
- Quick-reply buttons on `checkin_open` ("Start", "Not now"): they arrive as their own message type, which is read the same as typing the words, but this has not been seen from Meta.
- **Fixed (2 October): nothing a reply depends on is held in one copy's memory.** The request waiting for a yes or a no ("Shall I treat this as a leave request?") was held in the memory of the copy of the API that asked, so after a restart, or on a second copy, the yes or no went into the check-in as an answer. It is kept on the person's WhatsApp thread in the database now (migration `20261003090000_a_request_asked_about_on_whatsapp_survives_a_restart`): its kind, its words, which the yes hands to Ask Groundwork and the no puts in the check-in, and when it was asked. The words are cleared on the answer, and once ten minutes have passed, by the next message anybody sends on WhatsApp. After ten minutes a reply goes where it went before. The approver's said-back decision ("You are approving Amina's leave ... Is that right?") was already kept in the database, as a parked proposal, and so were the current check-in, the "which one?" list and the organisation picked on Groundwork's number. Running two copies still needs to be tried once hosted.

## The server settings WhatsApp needs

Names only, never values. Each is set where the API is hosted.

The full steps, with which settings are secret and how to connect the webhook, are in `docs/whatsapp-settings.md`.

| Setting | What it holds | Where it is found in Meta's tools |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN` | The permanent token Groundwork sends messages with. | Meta Business Settings, Users, System users: a system user with the WhatsApp permissions, then "Generate new token". |
| `WHATSAPP_PHONE_NUMBER_ID` | Meta's id for Groundwork's WhatsApp number (not the number itself). | The Meta app, WhatsApp, API Setup, "Phone number ID". |
| `WHATSAPP_APP_SECRET` | The secret that proves a message came from Meta. Without it, every incoming message is refused. | The Meta app, App settings, Basic, "App secret". |
| `WHATSAPP_WEBHOOK_VERIFY_TOKEN` | A word Groundwork and Meta agree on, so Meta can check the callback address once. Chosen by whoever sets it up. | Typed into the Meta app, WhatsApp, Configuration, "Verify token", next to the callback address. |
| `WHATSAPP_APPROVED_TEMPLATES` | The names of the templates Meta has approved, separated by commas. | WhatsApp Manager, Message templates: each name whose status is Active. |
| `WHATSAPP_DISPLAY_NUMBER` | Groundwork's WhatsApp number as people read it, for example +254 709 000 111. Not a secret. | WhatsApp Manager, Phone numbers. |
| `PUBLIC_API_URL` | The API's public address, without /api/v1. The callback address is this followed by /api/v1/webhooks/whatsapp. Not a secret. | Not in Meta: it is where the API is hosted. It is pasted into the Meta app, WhatsApp, Configuration, "Callback URL". |

WhatsApp also stays off until a Groundwork admin switches it on in the back office, even when all of these are set.
